Physical learning in adaptive networks

Physical networks — electrical, mechanical, or otherwise — can learn desired input-output responses by locally adjusting their own parameters, without an external controller. I study how this local adaptation couples a "cost" landscape, defined over the learnable parameters, to the network's own physical landscape (e.g. dissipated power), and what that coupling reveals about the function a network has learned.

Elastic network models of protein mutation and global epistasis

Working with the Liu group, I develop an elastic-network-model (ENM) pipeline that predicts how single mutations deform a protein's structure. Random-force ensembles applied at mutated residue sites give a predicted strain field through the Hessian's linear response, which we compare against structural strain computed from AlphaFold-relaxed mutant structures — connecting this deformation picture to global epistasis in protein fitness landscapes.
